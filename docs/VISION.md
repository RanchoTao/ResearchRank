# Vision

ResearchRank should be a research radar, not a “world scholar ranking.” It helps users map a well-defined field, inspect the evidence behind influence, discover groups and collaborators, recognize rising contributors, and identify topics whose activity and unmet needs suggest either overcrowding or opportunity.

## Principles

1. **Field-specific:** comparisons are valid only inside declared topic and time cohorts. Norms for theory, computer vision, and systems are not interchangeable.
2. **Evidence-based:** a score must lead back to versioned papers, venues, citations, repositories, datasets, benchmarks, awards, or institutional sources.
3. **Multi-dimensional and time-aware:** recent output, durable impact, adoption, growth, and curation remain separately visible.
4. **Group-aware:** labs, teams, collaborations, and advisor–student pipelines complement individual profiles.
5. **Strategically useful:** maps should serve topic selection, advisor and collaboration discovery, and opportunity analysis.
6. **Contestable:** records expose provenance, notes, uncertainty, and a correction process. A curator adjustment never silently overwrites evidence.

## From catalog to opportunity radar

A later radar can combine topic growth, concentration of groups, unresolved benchmark gaps, deployment constraints, and evidence freshness. It should display these signals rather than declare unexplored topics as facts. Timelines and graph views can reveal when a benchmark created a subfield, when repository adoption diverged from citations, and where demand rises faster than participation.

## Non-goals for v0

No universal ranking, exhaustive coverage, automated truth claims, heavy scraping, causal inference, or production UI. Seed organizations are examples and not an ordered recommendation.
